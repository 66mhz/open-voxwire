Inside Voxwire

01 / THE WHOLE PATH

From a murmur to an action

Nothing has to leave your computer. Your voice is captured, cleaned up, turned into text on-device, matched to something Voxwire knows how to do, and then — if that action could change anything — it stops and waits for you to say yes.

02 / THE CATCH

Why a throat mic needs a partner

A throat mic rests on your neck and reads your vocal cords through your skin. That is what makes it magic: it ignores room noise and works when you barely make a sound. But it is band-limited — it captures the low “voicing” that carries your vowels and drops the high-frequency consonants (s, f, t, k, sh) that speech recognition leans on hardest.

An ordinary air mic is the mirror image: it hears those consonants crisply — but it also hears the whole room. Neither mic alone is enough. The trick is to use each for the half it is good at.

03 / THE IDEA

Dual-mic fusion

Voxwire records both mics at once and combines them before transcribing. It splits the sound at a crossover around 900 Hz: keep the throat below it (clean, noise-immune voicing) and borrow the air mic above it (the consonants) — but only while the throat says you are actually speaking, so the air mic’s room noise stays out of the pauses around what you say. The gate holds open through the short gaps between words, so there the room comes through.

04 / THE HARD PART

Keeping the two mics in step

Two microphones only fuse cleanly if their recordings line up in time. There are two ways to get there — one solves it in hardware, the other in software.

One 2-channel device

A stereo interface or a macOS Aggregate Device records throat and air on a single shared clock — so the two channels are sample-aligned for free, with no drift. This is Voxwire’s preferred path, and it’s the “one tiny board, two conductors” idea taken to its cleanest form.

Two separate devices

Two independent mics start a few milliseconds apart. Voxwire lines them up in software by cross-correlating their shared voicing envelope — accurate to about a millisecond, more than enough for short push-to-talk commands. (Only long, continuous streams drift enough to need re-aligning.)

05 / WHAT’S BUILT

What's built

The fusion engine and its capture path are implemented and tested; the surrounding system is what carries a murmur all the way to a result.

DSP

Fusion engine

Band-merge + voicing gate + time-alignment, covered by unit tests on synthetic signals.

I/O

Aligned capture

Sample-aligned 2-channel capture, with a clean fall-back to a single mic.

LLM

Zero-config cleanup

Picks the best on-device model automatically; stays faithful and off by default.

UI

Simplified console

The daily controls up front; model, context, and fusion tucked into Advanced.

SVC

Always-on (macOS)

Runs at login via launchd — arm the mic whenever, no terminal needed.

STT

Pluggable + local

MLX on Apple Silicon, faster-whisper elsewhere; your audio needn’t leave the machine.

It can act — so it asks first

A microphone that can run commands is a real attack surface: anyone who can speak near it, or slip text into the agent, is trying to drive your machine. So the power to approve a destructive action lives with the executor, not the agent — a native dialog the agent cannot click for itself, denied by default. Voxwire is dictation-first today; tools stay off, host by host, until you grant them.
